Sleep, in plain words

Six things worth knowing

No jargon, no scare stories — just the bits that actually change how you sleep.

The 90-minute cycle

Why waking up mid-cycle feels like wading through mud.

Sleep runs in cycles of about 90 minutes: light sleep, deep sleep, then dream (REM) sleep. Waking at the end of a cycle — light sleep — feels easy. Waking from deep sleep feels awful and takes an hour to shake off. That is why 7h 30m often beats 8h 00m.

Caffeine's 5-hour half-life

Your 3 PM coffee is still half awake at 8 PM.

Caffeine halves in your body roughly every 5 hours. A 4 PM filter coffee (about 95 mg) is still ~48 mg at 9 PM — like half a cup, right when you are trying to wind down. The safe rule: last coffee 8 hours before bed, sooner if you are sensitive.

The 20-minute nap

Short naps sharpen you. Long naps backfire.

A 10–20 minute nap stays in light sleep, so you wake up clear-headed. Past 30 minutes you sink into deep sleep, and waking from it leaves you groggy for up to half an hour — sleep inertia. Nap before 3 PM, in a darkish room, and set a real alarm as backup.

Snoring: when to worry

Most snoring is harmless. Some of it is not.

Occasional light snoring after a heavy meal or a cold is normal. See a doctor if you snore loudly most nights, gasp or stop breathing in your sleep, or feel exhausted despite a full night — those are classic signs of sleep apnoea, which is common and very treatable.

Exam-night sleep

The all-nighter is a bad trade, every time.

Memory consolidates during sleep — especially deep sleep early in the night. An all-nighter trades the very hours your brain uses to file what you studied. A full night before an exam beats two extra hours of cramming, and it shows in the paper. Sleep 7h 30m, wake at your usual time, revise lightly.

Shift work & jet lag

Anchor sleep beats random sleep.

If your hours shift, keep one fixed 4-hour “anchor” sleep window every day — the same hours, no matter what. Light is the other lever: bright light when you need to be awake, darkness (and an eye mask) when you need to sleep. Melatonin is not a sleeping pill; talk to a doctor before using it.

The quiet hour

One calm hour before bed beats any sleep app.

Dim light, a paper book, warm milk. Boring on purpose — that is the whole point.
